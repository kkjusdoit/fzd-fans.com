### Newspaper Clipping: Oriental Sports Daily (Monday, August 31, 2026 - Page A10)

**Page Editor: Li Xiaochen**  
**Reporter: Cheng Chao**

> **Lead**: By a narrow margin of just two points, Fan Zhendong fell short of completing a monumental comeback. Two minor lapses in serve execution proved decisive. On August 29 (Beijing time), in the fourth rubber of the German Cup Round of 16 between Borussia Düsseldorf and Ochsenhausen, Fan suffered a heartbreaking 2-3 defeat. Behind this loss, a series of details intertwined with the number "2" weave a telling portrait of the cruelty and charm of competitive sports.

---

## No One Can Be the Savior in Every Match

Looking back at the gripping fourth rubber, it was a direct clash between the two clubs' aces: Fan Zhendong and Shunsuke Togami. In their two previous international meetings, Fan had won both. Their most recent clash was in last season's German Cup semifinal—also a decisive fourth rubber—where Fan defeated Togami 3-1 to guide Saarbrücken to victory.

In this rematch, Fan fell behind 0-2 early on, but mounted a resilient comeback to force a deciding fifth game. At the critical 10-all deuce, two serve executions saw vulnerabilities: a backhand serve was anticipated by his opponent, and another serve drifted long off the table, instantly tilting the momentum. Coach Wu Jingping analyzed the match on social media, pinpointing this micro detail: *"The only mistake Xiao Pang made was when he served long at 11-10 up in the decider, allowing the opponent to attack directly and level the score, which ultimately led to the defeat."*

Zooming out to the entire team match, the motif of "2" remained prominent. Düsseldorf twice leveled the team score only to remain two wins short. Anton Källberg lost to Togami in the opening match, and Dang Qiu fell to Iulian Chirita in the third match. With two teammates dropping matches, immense pressure rested squarely on Fan Zhendong's shoulders. The German Cup follows a single-elimination knockout format with no second chances. Trailing 1-2 overall, Fan faced zero margin for error in the fourth rubber—only a win could push the tie to a fifth and final match. Meanwhile, facing an opponent who had never beaten him before, encountering opponent peak performance is a natural part of competitive sports.

The number "2" holds another dimension: Fan's timeline abroad. On August 20, during his official unveiling in Düsseldorf, Fan remarked: *"This is my second year in Germany, and a brand new chapter in my career."* Last season with Saarbrücken, he steered the club to an unprecedented Treble (German Cup, Champions League, Bundesliga). Joining Düsseldorf on July 1 for his sophomore European season, he shoulders even higher public expectations while navigating a more complex situation: a new team lineup and the need to gradually adapt without sufficient high-intensity warmup fixtures. A narrow defeat does not signal a decline in capability; rather, it is a necessary trial in the process of adapting to a new environment and tuning competitive form.

Elimination is not an endpoint, but the starting line for adjustment and growth. With the German Cup campaign concluded, Düsseldorf enters an adjustment window. With the Bundesliga in full swing and the Champions League approaching, the club must confront several realities: first, Fan Zhendong needs more match play to sharpen his feel, but cannot be expected to play the savior in every single contest; second, Källberg needs to revitalize his tactical system to avoid losing rhythm in lower-intensity exchanges; third, Dang Qiu must conquer the psychological hurdle of closing out crunch points. The team must shift its winning reliance from individual brilliance to cohesive team strength.

## Domestic Theaters Broadcast German Cup Live: Fan Zhendong Becomes the Summer's "Biggest Dark Horse"

This German Cup club tournament held in Nuremberg unexpectedly sparked a nationwide craze for cinema group viewings in Shanghai and across China. Youku Sports partnered with Huaxia Film to launch an offline cinema viewing initiative, with an initial batch of 36 cinemas nationwide participating. In Shanghai, what began as 3 cinemas offering live broadcasts quickly expanded to 5 to meet passionate fan demand, with tickets essentially selling out across all screenings.

By August 29, despite accounting for less than 0.1% of total nationwide cinema screenings, the match broadcasts generated over 1.125 million RMB in box office revenue, boasting an average attendance rate of 88.7%—matching the buzz of advance preview screenings of blockbuster films like *Oppenheimer*. Fans flocked to cinemas to experience the action together on the giant screen, making this club fixture a true "dark horse" of the summer season. This phenomenon not only demonstrates Fan Zhendong's immense personal drawing power, but also highlights how table tennis is breaking beyond traditional television broadcasting into a younger, experiential form of consumption.

As massive attention flooded the arena, the heightened spotlight also amplified differing opinions surrounding victory and defeat. Following the match, two distinct sentiments emerged on social media: many fans were deeply moved by Fan Zhendong's relentless fight until the very last point, while others raised questions, escalating a narrow two-point loss in a club cup into debates about athletic decline.

In the face of defeat, public discourse calls for a rational sporting mindset. Grand Slam accolades reflect past glory, but no athlete can win every single match. Across the entire contest, a two-point margin, a two-game deficit overcome, two serve details, and a sophomore season abroad—details revolving around "2" painted a complete picture. While loss brings regret, it holds immense reflective value. For Fan Zhendong, who continues to compete overseas and constantly refine his game, every setback serves as a stepping stone toward an even stronger self.
